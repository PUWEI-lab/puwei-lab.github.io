吳柏葳 PUWEI WU
b. 1999, Keelung

新北淡水
Tamsui, New Taipei City

吳柏葳（b.1999，基隆），畢業於國立臺北藝術大學美術學系碩士班繪畫組。創作關注當代繪畫的操演手法，以及其回應影像經驗的可能性。創作分為側重空間意識的現地製作支線，以及在畫布內繪圖，使用影像的繪畫談論圖像歷史及文化認同的分支。

在現地繪製模糊人像的脈絡中，利用脫離框架的繪畫，在展覽及生活場域製造觀者與作品的不期而遇，同時藉由特定圖像之於空間的介入，試著回應作品、空間與觀眾之間的相互關係。2023年在新竹市鐵道藝術村辦理首次個展「看他一臉扁平」。2024年於日本黃金町駐村交流，在駐地創作期間，依照不同的展出地點因地制宜，從當地居民及藝術家人像的切入方式談論藝術與地方之間的連結性。近年將該創作脈絡的目光轉向具有台灣地方性及時代特色的產業之中，諸如青草行、委託行等，藉由創作的名義深入了解時代社會變遷之下的現實樣貌，並加以轉化。

在架上畫的創作支線中，關注畫布平面的幻象特性及顏料的物質性。以日本特攝片的角色身份為主題，試圖將繪畫手段分層執行，使用高工藝的製作方式回應特攝片複雜的拍攝方式。作品同時溯源怪獸及戰爭歷史的淵源，以臺灣人的身份，談論在過去殖民歷史下，臺灣人民的文化認同、恐懼與自我投射。曾獲第22屆台新藝術獎第二季提名及第24屆台新藝術獎第三季提名，並持續在繪畫創作中透過觀念與形式的操演，探索當代繪畫於不同載體、不同呈現方式的可能性。

Wu Puwei (b. 1999, Keelung) is a graduate of the MFA Painting Program in the Department of Fine Arts at Taipei National University of the Arts. His practice focuses on operative strategies in contemporary painting and their potential responses to image-based experiences. His work unfolds along two parallel trajectories: one centers on site-specific production with an emphasis on spatial awareness, while the other develops within the canvas, employing image-based painting to address image history and cultural identity.

Within his site-specific practice, Wu produces blurred portraits that break away from the conventional frame of painting. By situating these works in exhibition spaces and everyday environments, he creates unexpected encounters between viewers and artworks. Through the spatial intervention of specific images, he explores the interrelations among artwork, space, and audience. In 2023, he held his first solo exhibition, He Looks SUPERFLAT, at Art Site of Railway Warehouse, Hsinchu City. In 2024, he participated in an artist residency and exchange program in Koganecho, Japan. During the residency, he developed works in response to different exhibition sites, using portraits of residents and artists as points of entry to discuss the connections between art and locality. In recent years, this line of inquiry has shifted toward Taiwanese industries marked by strong local and historical characteristics—such as herbal medicine shops and commission agencies—through which he engages with the realities of social change, entering these contexts under the premise of artistic research and transforming his observations through painting.

In his studio-based painting practice, Wu investigates the illusory qualities of the canvas surface and the materiality of paint. Taking characters from Japanese tokusatsu films as his subject matter, he executes painting processes in layered stages, employing highly refined techniques to respond to the complex production methods of tokusatsu cinema. His works trace the historical entanglements between monsters and warfare, and, from the perspective of a Taiwanese artist, address cultural identity, collective fear, and self-projection shaped by Taiwan’s colonial past. Wu was nominated for the Second Quarter of the 22nd Taishin Arts Award and the Third Quarter of the 24th Taishin Arts Award. Through continuous explorations of concept and form, he seeks to expand the possibilities of contemporary painting across different media and modes of presentation.
